"""
KESTREL SHORTS — VOICE ENGINE
=============================

STATUS: PLACEHOLDER / DESIGN SPECIFICATION ONLY

## PURPOSE

This file documents the intended responsibilities, capabilities, vocabulary,
and implementation requirements of the future Kestrel Shorts Voice Engine.

It is documentation only. It must not load a TTS model, generate audio,
modify text, or execute voice-generation operations in its current state.

When implementation begins, this specification should guide the design of
the real Voice Engine and its interface with the Story Director.

## CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

The Story Director decides HOW a section should feel and be delivered.

The Voice Engine decides HOW to produce that delivery using available
text-to-speech backends, prosody controls, timing adjustments, and audio
processing techniques.

The Story Director must not need to know backend-specific settings such as
numeric speed multipliers, model parameters, pitch values, or pause durations
in milliseconds.

For example:

```
Story Director:
    delivery_style = "reflective"
    emotion = "curiosity"
    intensity = "restrained"
    pace = "measured"

Voice Engine:
    Interprets these directions and maps them to appropriate backend
    settings, text segmentation, emphasis, pauses, and other supported
    speech-generation techniques.
```

The mapping from creative direction to implementation must be owned by
the Voice Engine, not duplicated across the Story Director.

The Story Director should select from an explicit, documented vocabulary
of supported voice directions. The Voice Engine must validate those
directions and define how each one should sound.

============================================================

1. DELIVERY STYLES
   ============================================================

## PURPOSE

Delivery style describes the overall manner in which the narration is
performed. It is the broadest voice-direction category.

## PROPOSED OPTIONS

1. conversational
   Natural, approachable, and similar to a person explaining something
   directly to another person.

2. storytelling
   Narratively engaging delivery that guides the listener through events,
   discoveries, or a sequence of ideas.

3. reflective
   Thoughtful, measured delivery that gives ideas room to resonate.

4. dramatic
   Deliberate, expressive delivery intended to heighten the significance
   of an event, revelation, or narrative moment.

5. documentary
   Clear, composed, informative delivery suitable for factual narration.

6. investigative
   Focused delivery that conveys examination, questioning, or the gradual
   uncovering of information.

7. suspenseful
   Controlled delivery that creates anticipation without unnecessarily
   exaggerating the material.

8. intimate
   Close, personal delivery that feels direct and emotionally connected.

9. authoritative
   Confident, composed delivery suited to established explanations or
   clearly supported conclusions.

10. playful
    Light, expressive delivery suited to humorous, whimsical, or playful
    material.

11. urgent
    Forward-moving delivery that communicates time pressure or immediate
    importance.

12. solemn
    Restrained, serious delivery suited to tragic, grave, or weighty
    material.

13. soothing
    Gentle, calming delivery intended to reduce tension or create a relaxed
    listening experience.

14. energetic
    Lively delivery with a strong sense of momentum.

15. whispered
    A quiet or whisper-like delivery, only where the selected TTS backend
    can support it convincingly.

16. deadpan
    Deliberately understated, emotionally restrained delivery, often useful
    for dry humor or ironic narration.

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* These options are proposed vocabulary, not yet a final fixed list.
* Each option must have a clear definition and intended use.
* The Voice Engine must document how each style maps to supported controls.
* Different styles may share some implementation settings while retaining
  different creative meanings.
* A style must not promise an effect that the selected backend cannot
  produce.
* Unsupported styles must trigger a documented fallback rather than silently
  pretending that the requested effect was achieved.

============================================================
2. EMOTIONS
===========

## PURPOSE

Emotion describes the emotional quality that should be communicated through
the voice.

Emotion is distinct from delivery style. For example, documentary delivery
can communicate curiosity, sadness, wonder, or concern.

## PROPOSED OPTIONS

1. neutral
2. curiosity
3. wonder
4. excitement
5. joy
6. sadness
7. concern
8. fear
9. anger
10. frustration
11. determination
12. confidence
13. uncertainty
14. relief
15. nostalgia
16. admiration
17. empathy
18. amusement
19. anticipation
20. reverence
21. melancholy
22. hopefulness
23. surprise
24. tension
25. compassion

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Define each emotion in terms of its intended vocal impression.
* Distinguish emotion from intensity: sadness can be subtle or strong.
* Distinguish emotion from delivery style: a reflective passage can express
  hopefulness, melancholy, curiosity, or several of these across sections.
* Emotion should influence vocal delivery without changing the factual
  meaning of the script.
* Avoid automatically assigning strong emotional delivery to every sentence.
* Preserve emotional continuity across connected sections where appropriate.
* Do not assume that every emotion can be directly synthesized by every
  TTS backend. Some effects may require indirect techniques or may not be
  reproducible reliably.

============================================================
3. INTENSITY
============

## PURPOSE

Intensity describes how strongly the selected delivery style or emotion
should be expressed.

## PROPOSED OPTIONS

1. subtle
   Barely pronounced expressive coloring; natural and understated.

2. restrained
   Clearly intentional but controlled expression.

3. moderate
   Noticeable expression without dominating the narration.

4. strong
   Prominent expression suited to an important narrative moment.

5. extreme
   Highly pronounced expression, reserved for exceptional moments.

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Intensity must not be treated as a synonym for loudness.
* It may affect the strength of supported expressive controls, emphasis,
  pacing, or other appropriate techniques.
* Strong intensity must not automatically mean faster speech.
* Restrained intensity must not automatically mean whispering.
* Extreme intensity should be used sparingly.
* The engine must define safe and natural defaults for each style/emotion
  combination.

============================================================
4. PACE AND RHYTHM
==================

## PURPOSE

Pace describes the perceived speed and forward movement of the narration.

## PROPOSED OPTIONS

1. very_slow
   Deliberate delivery for exceptional emphasis, solemnity, or contemplation.

2. slow
   Slower delivery that allows the listener to absorb information.

3. measured
   Controlled, deliberate delivery with clear phrasing and natural space.

4. natural
   Balanced conversational pacing.

5. brisk
   Lively forward movement while retaining intelligibility.

6. fast
   Rapid delivery for suitable material that benefits from urgency or energy.

7. very_fast
   Exceptional rapid delivery; must not be used if intelligibility suffers.

## RELATED RHYTHM CONCEPTS

The engine should distinguish:

* Speaking pace: how quickly words are spoken.
* Phrase rhythm: how words are grouped and connected.
* Pausing: where speech stops and resumes.
* Articulation: how clearly and distinctly sounds and words are formed.
* Emphasis: which words or phrases receive additional attention.
* Energy: how much forward momentum the delivery conveys.

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* The Story Director chooses a descriptive pace, not a numeric speed value.
* The Voice Engine maps descriptive pace to backend-specific settings.
* Speed adjustments must respect the capabilities and limitations of each
  backend.
* Do not assume all TTS systems use the same speed scale or parameter.
* The engine should avoid extreme changes that make speech unnatural.
* Pace should be interpreted alongside delivery style, emotion, intensity,
  and sentence structure.
* A section's pace may be adjusted carefully around emphasis or pauses.
* Any automatic pace adjustment must preserve intelligibility.

============================================================
5. VOCAL CHARACTER AND TONAL QUALITY
====================================

## PURPOSE

This category describes the intended perceived character of the voice,
separate from the identity of the selected voice.

## PROPOSED DESCRIPTORS

* warm
* cool
* bright
* dark
* gentle
* firm
* airy
* crisp
* smooth
* rough
* resonant
* soft
* assertive
* breathy
* intimate
* formal
* relaxed
* expressive
* understated
* neutral

## IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

These are desired perceptual qualities, not guaranteed low-level controls.

For example, "warm" does not necessarily correspond to one pitch value,
and "bright" does not necessarily correspond to one equalization setting.

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Document which qualities can be produced directly by each backend.
* Document which qualities can only be approximated.
* Do not invent unsupported parameters merely because a descriptor exists.
* Avoid aggressive audio processing that changes voice identity or creates
  unnatural artifacts.
* Keep voice identity and vocal characterization as separate concepts.

============================================================
6. EMPHASIS
===========

## PURPOSE

Emphasis identifies a word or phrase that deserves additional listener
attention.

The Story Director should identify WHAT deserves emphasis. The Voice Engine
decides HOW to realize that emphasis.

## CONCEPTUAL STRUCTURE

Each emphasis instruction should contain:

* target: the exact word or phrase to emphasize
* strength: the desired degree of emphasis
* optional reason or semantic intent, if useful to the architecture

## PROPOSED STRENGTH OPTIONS

1. light
   Subtle emphasis that should not distract from the sentence.

2. moderate
   Noticeable emphasis that draws attention to the selected target.

3. strong
   Prominent emphasis reserved for particularly significant words or phrases.

## PROPOSED EMPHASIS TECHNIQUES

Depending on backend capabilities, emphasis may be achieved through:

* contextual text segmentation
* supported speech-style or emotion conditioning
* changes in local pacing
* changes in phrase boundaries
* controlled pauses around a target
* supported prosodic controls
* carefully selected punctuation, where appropriate
* backend-specific expressive controls
* optional audio processing, if safe and useful

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Verify that the target actually occurs in the spoken text.
* Handle repeated target phrases unambiguously.
* Define whether matching is case-sensitive.
* Define how punctuation adjacent to a target is handled.
* Do not silently apply emphasis to a different word if matching fails.
* Limit excessive emphasis instructions within one section.
* Avoid applying several competing techniques to the same word without
  a defined precedence rule.
* Preserve the original spoken wording unless a separate text-editing
  stage explicitly authorizes a change.
* Do not insert spoken meta-instructions such as "emphasize this word."
* If a backend cannot reliably emphasize a target, use a documented fallback
  or report that the effect is unsupported.

============================================================
7. PAUSES AND SILENCE
=====================

## PURPOSE

Pause instructions control where intentional silence should occur and what
that silence is meant to accomplish.

The Story Director chooses a meaningful pause location and its intended
length category. The Voice Engine translates this into actual timing.

## PROPOSED PAUSE LOCATIONS

1. before_phrase
2. after_phrase
3. before_sentence
4. after_sentence
5. between_sentences
6. before_emphasis
7. after_emphasis
8. before_revelation
9. after_revelation
10. between_narrative_beats

These are semantic positions. The implementation must define exactly how
each position is anchored to the spoken text.

## PROPOSED PAUSE DURATIONS

1. micro
   A very brief separation, where a small break is sufficient.

2. short
   A light pause that helps phrase boundaries feel natural.

3. medium
   A clearly perceptible pause that allows an idea to settle.

4. long
   An extended pause for a meaningful dramatic, emotional, or narrative beat.

5. extended
   A rare, substantial silence used only when the scene truly benefits.

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* The Story Director must not specify milliseconds.
* The Voice Engine defines the actual duration associated with each category.
* Duration mappings may vary by delivery style or backend.
* The engine must define precedence when pause instructions overlap.
* The engine must define how pauses interact with punctuation and natural
  pauses already produced by the TTS model.
* Avoid creating awkward double pauses.
* Define whether a pause is inserted before or after the exact matched text.
* Define behavior when a pause anchor cannot be found.
* Keep the final audio duration within reasonable limits.
* Avoid excessive pauses that make narration feel fragmented.
* Explicit silence must be distinguishable from naturally occurring silence
  in the generated audio where possible.

============================================================
8. PRONUNCIATION AND ARTICULATION
=================================

## PURPOSE

This category concerns the clarity and manner in which spoken words are
articulated.

## PROPOSED DESCRIPTORS

* natural
* crisp
* precise
* relaxed
* deliberate
* conversational
* careful
* softened

## POTENTIAL CAPABILITIES

The future implementation may support:

* pronunciation dictionaries
* custom pronunciations for names and unusual terms
* phonetic overrides where supported
* language and locale selection
* acronym pronunciation rules
* number, date, and abbreviation normalization
* pronunciation consistency across a complete video
* preservation of character and place-name pronunciations
* validation of text normalization before synthesis

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Do not alter factual wording simply to make pronunciation easier.
* Keep spoken-text normalization separate from script content.
* Preserve a traceable relationship between source text and spoken output.
* Avoid inconsistent pronunciation of the same name across sections.
* Document which features belong to text preprocessing and which belong
  to the TTS backend.

============================================================
9. CHARACTER VOICE IDENTITY
===========================

## PURPOSE

Voice identity defines WHO is speaking. Delivery style and emotion define
HOW that voice speaks in a particular section.

Potential identity attributes include:

* registered voice or speaker ID
* selected TTS voice
* reference audio, where supported and authorized
* language and locale
* stable pronunciation preferences
* stable character-specific vocal traits
* backend compatibility requirements

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Use the existing character and registry architecture where applicable.
* Do not hardcode channel-specific character identities inside generic
  prosody logic.
* Do not treat emotion or delivery style as a replacement for speaker identity.
* Keep a character's stable voice identity consistent across sections.
* Allow moment-to-moment emotional and delivery changes without unintentionally
  changing the identity of the speaker.
* Validate that the chosen voice is available to the selected backend.
* Define clear fallback behavior when a voice is unavailable.

============================================================
10. BACKEND CAPABILITIES AND ADAPTERS
=====================================

## PURPOSE

The Voice Engine should expose one stable, backend-independent interface to
the rest of Kestrel Shorts while allowing different TTS systems to have
different implementation details.

## PLANNED BACKENDS

Primary: F5-TTS
Fallback: Kokoro

These are architectural preferences. Their exact interfaces and capabilities
must be inspected and verified during implementation.

## REQUIRED DESIGN

1. Backend-independent voice directions

   The Story Director should use shared descriptors such as "reflective",
   "curiosity", "restrained", "measured", "moderate", and "medium".

2. Backend-specific adapters

   Each backend adapter should translate supported directions into the
   actual parameters and operations available to that backend.

3. Capability declaration

   Each backend should declare which features it supports directly, which
   it can approximate, and which it cannot support.

4. Safe fallback

   Unsupported directions must degrade gracefully or produce a clear
   diagnostic. They must not be silently misrepresented as fully supported.

5. Consistent external interface

   The Story Director should not need separate instructions for F5-TTS and
   Kokoro.

6. Capability-aware behavior

   The engine should know whether a feature can be implemented by the current
   backend before attempting to apply it.

## POTENTIAL CAPABILITY CATEGORIES

* voice selection
* voice/reference conditioning
* language and locale
* delivery-style interpretation
* emotion interpretation
* intensity
* pace and speed
* emphasis
* pause insertion
* pronunciation overrides
* text segmentation
* speaker consistency
* audio concatenation
* silence insertion
* output format and sample rate
* duration measurement
* audio-quality checks
* diagnostics and error reporting

Do not assume that every backend implements these capabilities in the same
way. Confirm them from the actual installed API and inference implementation.

============================================================
11. COMBINING VOICE DIRECTIONS
==============================

## PURPOSE

The Voice Engine must define how several creative directions interact.

## EXAMPLE

```
delivery_style: reflective
emotion: curiosity
intensity: restrained
pace: measured
```

These instructions should combine into a coherent delivery rather than four
independent effects fighting each other.

## IMPORTANT COMBINATIONS TO CONSIDER

* style + emotion
* emotion + intensity
* pace + pauses
* pace + emphasis
* emphasis + pauses
* character identity + emotion
* character identity + delivery style
* local emphasis + overall pace
* narrative continuity across adjacent sections
* section-level directions + phrase-level directions

## IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

* Define precedence when instructions conflict.
* Define safe defaults for omitted fields.
* Prevent unsupported or contradictory combinations where appropriate.
* Avoid mechanically applying every available effect.
* Apply only those local effects that serve the intended delivery.
* Preserve intelligibility and naturalness as primary constraints.
* Allow restrained variation between sections without unnecessary inconsistency.

============================================================
12. DIRECTION SCOPE
===================

The future interface should distinguish between three levels.

A. SECTION-LEVEL DIRECTIONS

These define the overall delivery of a script section.

Examples:

* delivery style
* dominant emotion
* intensity
* pace
* broad vocal characterization

B. PHRASE-LEVEL DIRECTIONS

These affect a specific phrase or local narrative moment.

Examples:

* emphasis target
* local pause
* brief change in delivery
* localized expressive instruction

C. WORD-LEVEL DIRECTIONS

These are fine-grained instructions and should be used sparingly.

Examples:

* emphasis on a particular word
* custom pronunciation for a specific term
* a precisely anchored pause around a word

The Story Director should generally work at the section level and add local
instructions only when they materially improve the narration.

The Voice Engine should own the detailed implementation of those directions.

============================================================
13. VALIDATION AND DEFAULTS
===========================

The eventual implementation must validate all incoming voice directions.

Required checks include:

* Is the requested delivery style supported by the schema?
* Is the requested emotion supported by the schema?
* Is the intensity value valid?
* Is the pace value valid?
* Is the emphasis strength valid?
* Is the pause location valid?
* Is the pause duration valid?
* Does the emphasis target occur in the text?
* Can the pause anchor be resolved?
* Does the selected backend support or approximate the requested features?
* Are any instructions contradictory or redundant?
* Are all required fields present?
* Can missing optional fields safely use defaults?

Validation must happen in code, not solely in the Story Director's prompt.

The engine should distinguish:

* invalid directions
* valid directions unsupported by a backend
* valid directions approximated by a backend
* successfully applied directions
* directions ignored because they were unnecessary or redundant

Where useful, diagnostics should explain what happened.

============================================================
14. AUDIO QUALITY AND SAFETY
============================

The final engine should prioritize:

* intelligibility
* natural-sounding speech
* consistent voice identity
* correct text-to-audio correspondence
* stable pronunciation
* absence of unintended clipping
* absence of obvious segmentation artifacts
* appropriate handling of inserted silence
* reasonable audio duration
* consistent output format
* reliable file generation
* useful diagnostic information when generation fails

Audio post-processing must not be applied automatically unless it serves a
defined purpose and has been tested for artifacts.

If multiple audio segments are generated, the engine must handle transitions,
sample rates, channel layouts, silence, and final file assembly consistently.

============================================================
15. PROPOSED INTERFACE WITH THE STORY DIRECTOR
==============================================

The final interface should use a structured voice-direction object rather
than free-form implementation instructions.

## CONCEPTUAL EXAMPLE ONLY

{
"delivery_style": "reflective",
"emotion": "curiosity",
"intensity": "restrained",
"pace": "measured",
"vocal_quality": ["warm", "understated"],
"emphasis": [
{
"target": "another world",
"strength": "moderate"
}
],
"pauses": [
{
"anchor": "after_sentence",
"duration": "medium"
}
]
}

This example is a design sketch, not a finalized data contract.

Before integrating it with the Story Director, define:

* the authoritative field names
* required versus optional fields
* permitted values
* defaults
* target and anchor matching rules
* validation behavior
* versioning strategy
* backend capability reporting
* error and fallback behavior

The Voice Engine's schema must be the single source of truth consumed by
the Story Director. Do not maintain independent, conflicting lists of
allowed values in multiple files.

============================================================
16. FUTURE IMPLEMENTATION PLAN
==============================

When development begins:

1. Inspect the current TTS integrations and the exact installed APIs.
2. Finalize the shared voice-direction schema.
3. Define a backend-independent Voice Engine contract.
4. Implement the F5-TTS adapter.
5. Implement the Kokoro fallback adapter.
6. Implement validation, capability reporting, and diagnostics.
7. Implement section-level voice directions.
8. Add phrase-level emphasis and pause handling.
9. Test supported directions with representative scripts.
10. Calibrate backend-specific mappings through actual listening tests.
11. Integrate the schema into the Story Director.
12. Verify consistent voice identity and delivery across adjacent sections.
13. Document which features are native, approximated, or unsupported.

Do not finalize numeric mappings until the relevant backend interfaces and
generated audio have been tested.

============================================================
17. DESIGN RULES TO PRESERVE
============================

* The Story Director chooses creative intent.
* The Voice Engine owns voice-generation implementation.
* Shared voice directions must use a controlled vocabulary.
* The schema must be the single source of truth.
* Backend-specific controls must remain behind backend adapters.
* Never assume a backend supports a feature without verification.
* Preserve spoken text unless a separate stage authorizes changes.
* Keep voice identity distinct from delivery style and emotion.
* Prefer a small number of meaningful directions over excessive micromanagement.
* Apply emphasis and pauses selectively.
* Preserve intelligibility and naturalness.
* Validate directions in code.
* Use explicit fallback behavior and useful diagnostics.
* Keep this placeholder documentation-only until the actual implementation
  is deliberately started.
